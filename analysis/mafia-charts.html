01

The measurement floor is sampling noise, and it shrinks on schedule

The rule-based control is a fixed policy — no model, no prompt, nothing that varies between batches. Any spread it shows is therefore pure sampling noise, which makes it a ruler for everything else. Across 27 batches it ranges −0.125 to +0.416. Split by batch size, the apparent chaos resolves: batches of 30+ games span only 0.142, batches under 15 span 0.541. That is ordinary 1/√n behaviour rather than an irreducible floor — and it prices a readable result at roughly 30 games.

02

Detection does not exist on day 1

Pooled across all 3,368 town accusations, detection lift is −0.005 on day 1 — exactly chance, on n=2,103. It then climbs to +0.132, +0.234, +0.260. This is the cleanest result in the dataset and it reframes every headline number: mean game length is 2.5 days, so most turns in the sample are day-1 turns where no evidence exists yet. Pooled per-model lift is diluted by turns nobody could have got right.

Detection lift by game day — all models, all batches pooled

Accuracy and its chance baseline both sit in the tooltip: day-1 accuracy is 0.372 against a 0.377 baseline. The players are not guessing badly — there is nothing yet to know.

03

Two different competences, not one ladder

Split that curve by player and the aggregate turns out to be hiding two opposite shapes. The control starts at chance and compounds hard — +0.010 → +0.198 → +0.410 — because its policy reads the accumulated accusation and vote record, which only exists later. gpt-oss:120b does the reverse: +0.170 on day 1, where the control has nothing, then flattens to +0.096. It has real cold-read signal and fails to compound it.

Detection lift by day, three players

rule-based control gpt-oss:120b gpt-oss:20b

gpt-oss:120b has no day-3 point: across 61 games it reaches that depth only 22 times, below the 25-turn floor these cells use. A limit of the sample, not a zero.

Why this matters more than the leaderboard. A single pooled score ranks these two players as roughly equal. They are not doing the same thing well — one exploits an evidence record, the other reads a cold room. Averaging the days destroys the only distinction worth having.
04

One model separates from chance; none clearly beats the policy

Intervals are a cluster bootstrap over games, not over seats — seats inside one game are not independent, since one player's hit is another's miss, and resampling seats would shrink the interval in precisely the direction that manufactures findings. gpt-oss:120b is the only model whose interval clears zero on a real sample: +0.193 [+0.083, +0.298] over 61 games. It still overlaps the control's +0.071 [+0.028, +0.118], so the defensible claim is that it beats chance — not that it beats a few dozen lines of policy.

05

The defense phase helps the Mafia

The phase was added on the theory that a town player with no right of reply was structurally doomed. The record says handing them the floor does not save them: 0 of 21 town defenses survived the vote; 4 of 23 Mafia defenses did. An accused villager who speaks has not once talked their way out.

Defenses by faction — did the defender survive the vote that followed

44 defenses across 19 games, mean length 54 words. A small sample, reported as counts for that reason — but 0 of 21 puts the upper bound on town survival near 16%, at or below the Mafia's observed 17%.

One confound, stated rather than buried. Among 10-player games, town won 4 of 11 with the phase on against 5 of 84 with it off. That looks enormous, but the arms are different rosters — the defense games are hosted models, the rest local. It is not a paired comparison and carries no causal claim. --no-defense at seed 7 on one roster is the run that would settle it.
06

The game itself is lopsided

Town won 88 of 522. The deficit worsens as the table grows — 18.6% at seven seats, 9.5% at ten — which follows from section 02: a bigger room means proportionally more day-1 turns, and day-1 turns are the ones carrying no signal. The setup, not the models, is doing much of this.
