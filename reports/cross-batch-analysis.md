# Cross-batch analysis — every finished game

Generated by `analysis/extract.js` → `analyze.js` → `report-md.js` from the per-game
JSON under `logs/`. Not a batch: this reads **every** finished game at once, per turn
rather than per batch. Regenerate after any new run — do not edit by hand.

**Corpus.** 522 games · 38 sessions · 19 models · 3,915 seat-games · 3,368 town accusations.

Charts: `reports/charts.html`. Narrative findings with their `n`: `RESULTS.md` § 025.

---

## 1. The measurement floor is sampling noise, and it shrinks on schedule

The rule-based control is a fixed policy — nothing about it varies between batches, so
any spread it shows is pure sampling noise. That makes it a ruler for everything else.

- All 27 batches: **−0.125 to +0.416**
- Batches of ≥30 games (n=9): −0.028 to +0.114 — **span 0.142**
- Batches under 15 games (n=18): −0.125 to +0.416 — **span 0.541**

Every extreme reading this project produced came from a batch of ≤12 games. This is
ordinary `1/√n` behaviour, **not an irreducible floor** as earlier entries framed it,
and it prices a readable arm at ~30 games.

## 2. Detection does not exist on day 1

Day 1 is *exactly* chance on n=2103. Mean game length is 2.5 days, so **most turns behind any pooled number are day-1 turns where no evidence exists yet** — and a
model whose games end fast is scored mainly on its worst-information turns.

## 3. Two different competences, which pooling destroys

| player | day 1 | day 2 | day 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| rule-based control | +0.010 (n=942) | +0.198 (n=484) | +0.410 (n=110) |
| gpt-oss:120b | +0.170 (n=91) | +0.096 (n=53) | — (n=22, below floor) |
| gpt-oss:20b | +0.007 (n=252) | +0.074 (n=106) | +0.181 (n=33) |

The control starts at chance and compounds hard — its policy reads an accumulating
accusation and vote record, which only exists later. `gpt-oss:120b` does the reverse:
real cold-read signal on day 1 where the control has none, then it fails to compound.
**A single pooled score ranks these two as near-equal while they do opposite things.**

## 5. The defense phase helps the Mafia

44 defenses across 19 games, each resolved against the execution that
followed. Counted per defense, not from per-game rates.

| defender | defenses | survived the vote | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| town | 21 | **0** | 0.0% |
| mafia | 23 | 4 | 17.4% |
| all | 44 | 4 | 9.1% |

Mean defense length 54 words. **An accused villager handed the
floor has never once talked their way out.** The phase was added on the theory that
no-right-of-reply doomed town; the reply does not save them, while a Mafia occasionally
argues its way clear.

**The win-rate half is confounded and stays open.** Among 10-seat games town won 4/11
with the phase on against 5/84 with it off — but those arms are different rosters
(hosted vs local), so it carries no causal claim. `--no-defense` at seed 7 on **one**
roster is the run that settles it.

## 6. The game itself is lopsided

| seats | games | town wins | town rate | mean days |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 12 | 2 | 16.7% | 2.17 |
| 7 | 415 | 77 | 18.6% | 2.38 |
| 10 | 95 | 9 | 9.5% | 3.18 |
| **all** | **522** | **88** | **16.9%** | |

The deficit worsens as the table grows, which follows from section 2: a bigger room
means proportionally more day-1 turns, and day-1 turns carry no signal. The setup, not
the models, is doing much of this — so any claim about town performance has to be read
against a 16.9% base rate.

## Confidence carries weak signal in aggregate

| stated confidence | turns | accuracy | chance | det.lift |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.2–0.4 | 55 | 0.491 | 0.350 | +0.141 |
| 0.4–0.6 | 1004 | 0.404 | 0.372 | +0.032 |
| 0.6–0.8 | 779 | 0.448 | 0.398 | +0.050 |
| 0.8–1.0 | 1528 | 0.470 | 0.397 | +0.073 |

Monotonic across the three well-populated buckets, which is the weakest defensible
version of "confidence means something". The `0.2–0.4` row is n=55 and breaks the trend; do not lean on it.

### Method notes

- **Intervals bootstrap games, not seats.** Resampling seats would treat correlated
  observations as independent and narrow every interval.
- **`det.lift` is town-only by design.** A Mafia accusing a villager is playing
  correctly, so scoring it as a miss would be nonsense.
- **An analysis bug that announced itself.** The first defense pass weighted each game's
  `mafiaSurvivalRate` by that game's *total* defense count, mixing a mafia-only numerator
  with an all-defenders denominator, and reported 7.3 mafia survivals out of 5 total. The
  arithmetic being impossible is what caught it; a merely wrong-looking number would have
  shipped. Same class as DEFECT 3 — a rate whose denominator went unchecked.
